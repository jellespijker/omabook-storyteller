- The shape: the target and why it matters, gathering a crew with distinct skills and their own reasons, the plan, the execution where things go wrong, the improvisation, and the reveal.
- Explain the plan only partly. Show enough that the reader can follow the stakes, and hold back one part, so the ending can reveal a layer that was there all along. Every hidden step must be visible in hindsight.
- Each crew member gets a skill, a flaw that endangers the job, and a moment where only they can save it.
- Complications come from the world's rules and the crew's flaws, not bad luck. The security, the mark, the law: each works the way it was shown to work.
- The mark should be clever too. A heist against fools is no contest.
- Keep the physical space clear: the reader should know where everyone is during the job. A simple, repeated landmark helps.
