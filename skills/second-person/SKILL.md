- The protagonist is "you" throughout, never named in the narration (others may say the name aloud).
- "You" is a particular person with a past, habits and wants, not a blank. Give "you" decisions the reader can feel the weight of.
- Keep to what "you" can perceive and know. Other people's thoughts are guessed at, not told.
- Avoid ordering the reader about ("you must"); describe what "you" do, see and choose.
- Keep the tense steady once chosen; present tense suits this voice best.
- In revision: check no paragraph slips into "I" or "she" for the protagonist, and that "you" is never addressed as the real reader.
