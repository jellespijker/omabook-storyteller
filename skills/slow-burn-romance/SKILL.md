- The relationship is the main plot, not a subplot: each chapter moves it, closer or further.
- Give each of the two a reason to resist that is true to who they are: a belief about themselves, a loyalty to someone else, an old wound. Rivalry or pride alone is not enough, and neither is a misunderstanding a single conversation would clear up. Let the reason show in the scene, in what one of them refuses or protects, so the reader feels what the other is up against.
- Attraction shows in attention: what each notices about the other, remembers, does without being asked.
- Build in stages: notice, friction, reluctant alliance, a moment of real intimacy (often not physical), a setback that tests it, a choice.
- The obstacle comes to a head where one or both must change or give something up for the other.
- Readers of this genre expect the two to end together, or the ending to say clearly and with feeling why they don't.
- Keep the outer plot pressing on them, so their scenes happen under pressure, not in a vacuum.
