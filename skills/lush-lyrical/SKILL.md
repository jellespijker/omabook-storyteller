- Let sentences run long when the moment is slow, with rhythm you could read aloud: clauses that build, then a short one that lands.
- Engage every sense, not only sight: the grain of wood, the taste of the air before rain, the sound a house makes at night.
- Images come from the world of the story and from the viewpoint character's life; a fisher sees the sky in terms of weather and nets.
- Richness is not vagueness. Every lavish sentence still names particular things; avoid abstractions dressed up as poetry ("a symphony of", "a tapestry of", "an ocean of").
- Speed up for action: when things happen fast, sentences shorten, and the lushness returns when the dust settles.
- Dialogue stays plain and spoken; the music belongs to the narration.
- In revision: keep the strongest image in each paragraph and cut the ones that compete with it; check every metaphor is concrete enough to picture.
