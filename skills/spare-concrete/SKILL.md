- Most sentences carry one action or one image. Vary the length now and then so the rhythm doesn't go flat, but let short sentences be the norm.
- Choose the exact noun and the strong verb over an adjective and an adverb: "she shouldered the door", not "she pushed the door hard".
- Name feelings rarely. Show what a person does with their hands, what they look at, what they don't say.
- One telling detail per moment. Cut the second and third.
- No similes that need explaining. A comparison, when there is one, comes from the world of the story and the person seeing it.
- Leave things out. The reader can infer what follows from what is shown; trust them with the gap.
- In revision: cut filler ("began to", "seemed to", "very", "just", "suddenly"), and any sentence that repeats what the last one showed.
