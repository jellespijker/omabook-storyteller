- Plot the main problem as a series of attempts. Each attempt fails in one of two ways: "yes, but" (they get what they wanted, and it brings a new problem) or "no, and" (they fail, and things get worse).
- Every failure must teach the character something or close off an option, so the next attempt is different, not a repeat.
- Before the climax there are usually two or three failed tries; a problem solved on the first try feels unearned.
- The final attempt succeeds (or fails meaningfully) because of what the earlier failures taught, not by luck or rescue.
- In each scene: what does the viewpoint character want right now, what stands in the way, and how does it turn out? End most scenes on a setback.
- After a big failure, give a short beat of reaction: the character feels it, weighs what is left, and decides what to try next.
