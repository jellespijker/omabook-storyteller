  title: "Clear inventions"
  kind: craft
  stages: world, draft, revise
  cues: "clearer, less confusing, easier to follow, explain more, too many words"
---
- Introduce at most one or two invented terms in a scene, and only those the scene needs.
- The first time an invented thing appears, show it in use, with its effect and its cost, so its meaning is plain from context. A short clause of plain explanation is fine when it comes from the character's point of view.
- Prefer ordinary words with a twist over coined words: "the salt-tax" is clearer than a new noun.
- Use one name for one thing. Do not alternate between an invented term and its synonym.
- Reintroduce a term with a reminder if it has not appeared for several chapters.
- A rule of the world that will solve a problem later must be shown, with its limits, well before it does.
- In revision: underline every invented word and check each is clear from the page alone, to someone who has read nothing else.
