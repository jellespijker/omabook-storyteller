- Begin in the ordinary and make it specific, so the reader notices when something small is off.
- Escalate by small wrongnesses that keep happening: a sound at the same hour, an object moved, a person who knows something they shouldn't.
- What is not shown frightens more than what is. Keep the threat partly offstage, and describe effects rather than the thing itself.
- Characters behave sensibly. They check, they try to leave, they tell someone; the horror is that sensible things don't work.
- Let the rules of the threat be consistent even when unexplained; the reader should feel there is a logic they could learn.
- Quiet scenes are where dread builds; don't fill every gap with incident.
- Readers of this genre expect the threat to be real, a cost to be paid, and an ending that does not explain everything away.
