- Cut throat-clearing and emphasis crutches: "it was clear that", "in that moment", "somehow", "truly", "just", "very", "began to", "seemed to". Keep an adverb only when the verb cannot carry it.
- Break formulas. The contrast that denies one thing to announce another ("not X, but Y", "It wasn't X. It was Y.", "she wasn't afraid; it was something older") is the commonest tell of all, in any of its spellings: say what the thing is, and drop what it isn't. The same for a list of three, a dramatic one-word fragment, a rhetorical question that answers itself.
- Prefer a person doing something. Use the passive only when the actor is unknown or beside the point. Be wary of objects doing human things ("the silence demanded", "the room held its breath").
- Be specific. Replace a vague claim ("something had changed") with the thing that changed. Replace "every", "always", "never" when they are doing vague work.
- Vary the rhythm. Three sentences of the same length in a row: break one. Not every paragraph ends on a short punchy line; most should not.
- Cut the quotable. A sentence that sounds like a pull-quote or a moral is usually the narration explaining what the scene already showed.
- Use dashes sparingly; most can be a full stop, a comma or a colon.
- Change only what these find. A plain sentence that works stays as it is.
