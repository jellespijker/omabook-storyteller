- The humour is in the gap between what people say and what they do, and between how a character sees themselves and how the narration shows them.
- Understate. A disaster described calmly is funnier than one described loudly.
- Precise, unexpected word choice beats a punchline. One surprising verb can carry a paragraph.
- Never wink at the reader or undercut a real emotion with a joke when it matters; let a serious moment be serious, and the wryness return after.
- People in the story are not trying to be funny. They are trying to get what they want, and the situation is what's absurd.
- Keep the rhythm of a joke: set up plainly, turn on the last word of the sentence.
- In revision: cut any joke that explains itself, and any that the characters would not notice or care about.
